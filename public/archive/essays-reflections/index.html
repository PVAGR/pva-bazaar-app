INTRODUCTION: THE FRAGMENTS

Not every insight comes as a complete treatise. Some truths arrive as fragments—quick downloads, sudden clarity, brief illuminations.

This entry preserves those moments:

  • Blog posts never published
  • Journal entries too personal to share then
  • Reflections that didn't fit elsewhere

Each section stands alone, yet all connect to the larger tapestry.

ESSAY 1: THE TRAP OF SEEKING VALIDATION

Written During Hermit Period, Age 26

I caught myself today doing something insidious:

Mentally rehearsing how I'd explain my work to someone who might be impressed.

Not explaining to help them understand. Not sharing because it was relevant. But performing in my imagination for validation.

This is ego's most subtle trap.

The Pattern

You do meaningful work. You have genuine insights. You create something valuable.

Then ego says: "But what if no one recognizes it? What if they think you're delusional? You should make sure they understand how important this is."

So you:

  • Craft the perfect explanation
  • Anticipate their objections
  • Prepare your defense
  • Practice your pitch

You've shifted from doing the work to performing the work.

The Poison

Once validation becomes the goal:

  • The work becomes less pure
  • You create for audience, not truth
  • You seek recognition, not service
  • You need others' approval to feel valuable

Your power leaks out.

You've made them the authority over your truth.

The Cure

Do the work for its own sake.

If it's valuable, those meant to find it will find it. If it's true, those resonating with it will recognize it. If it's your dharma, the outcome isn't your concern.

Krishna's teaching in Bhagavad Gita: "You have a right to perform your duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions."

Translation: Do your work excellently. Release attachment to recognition. Let the results unfold as they will.

The Practice

When I catch myself seeking validation:

1. Notice without judgment "Ah, there's ego wanting approval."

2. Return to the work "What actually needs doing right now?"

3. Ask the deeper question "Am I doing this because it's true, or because I want credit?"

4. Recommit "This is my dharma whether anyone sees it or not."

The paradox: Once you stop seeking validation, people naturally validate you (because your work becomes more authentic).

But by then, you won't need it.

ESSAY 2: ON BEING "TOO MUCH"

Written After Social Gathering, Age 27

People have always told me I'm "too much":

Too intense. Too deep. Too serious. Too passionate. Too idealistic. Too everything.

For years, I tried to be less.

Dimmed my light. Dumbed down my speech. Hid my enthusiasm. Pretended to care about small talk.

It was exhausting.

The Realization

I'm not "too much."

I'm "too much" for people living "too little."

There's nothing wrong with depth in a culture addicted to shallow. There's nothing wrong with passion in a world that values cool detachment. There's nothing wrong with caring in an age of ironic nihilism.

The problem isn't me—it's the world's diminished capacity for aliveness.

The Shift

I stopped apologizing for my intensity.

If my depth makes people uncomfortable, they can:

  • Rise to meet me
  • Choose not to engage
  • Continue living shallow

Not my problem.

I'm done contorting myself to fit spaces too small for my soul.

For Others Who Are "Too Much"

You are not broken.

You are:

  • Alive in a numbed world
  • Awake in a sleeping culture
  • Passionate in an apathetic society
  • Real in a world of facades

Don't shrink.

The world needs people who are "too much":

  • Too loving (we're drowning in indifference)
  • Too honest (we're suffocating in lies)
  • Too hopeful (we're dying from cynicism)
  • Too alive (we're barely existing)

Be too much.

Unapologetically.

The right people won't think you're too much. They'll say, "Finally, someone who gets it."

ESSAY 3: THE LONELINESS OF SEEING

Written During Deep Meditation Retreat, Age 26

There's a particular loneliness that comes with spiritual awakening:

The loneliness of seeing what others don't yet see.

You see:

  • The illusions everyone believes
  • The systems everyone serves
  • The potential everyone ignores
  • The truth everyone fears

And you can't unsee it.

The Isolation

You try to share what you've seen.

They think you're:

  • Arrogant ("You think you know better than everyone?")
  • Crazy ("That's not how reality works")
  • Misguided ("You just need to be more practical")
  • Offensive ("How dare you question our way of life")

So you stop sharing.

And the loneliness deepens.

Not because you're alone in the room. But because you're alone in your perception of reality.

The Temptation

Two ways to escape this loneliness:

Option 1: Go back to sleep

  • Pretend you don't see what you see
  • Rejoin consensus reality
  • Stop questioning
  • Numb out

Option 2: Find your tribe

  • Seek others who see
  • Build community of the awakened
  • Support each other
  • Don't carry it alone

Option 1 is death. Option 2 is life.

But Option 2 is hard because your tribe is scattered, often hidden, and you must find each other.

The Faith

You must have faith that:

Others have seen what you've seen. Your tribe exists. You will find each other. You're not alone.

And sometimes:

Your loneliness is preparation.

You're being forged in solitude so that when you do find your tribe, you're strong enough to serve them.

The loneliness has purpose.

The Message

To those feeling this loneliness:

I see you. I know the isolation. I've lived it.

You're not crazy. You're not alone. We're finding each other.

The Taur Community exists for this—to end the loneliness of seeing.

ESSAY 4: WHY I DON'T DRINK ANYMORE

Written After Friend Questioned My Choices, Age 27

People ask why I stopped drinking.

Not because I was alcoholic (I wasn't). Not because of religion (I'm not dogmatic). Not because I judge those who do (I don't).

But because:

Reason 1: Consciousness is My Most Valuable Asset

Everything I've built came from:

  • Meditation
  • Clear thinking
  • Divine downloads
  • Subtle perceptions

Alcohol dulls all of this.

Even small amounts:

  • Reduce dream recall
  • Dampen intuition
  • Cloud meditation
  • Lower vibration

I can't afford that.

My visions, my purpose, my work—all require maximum consciousness.

Sobriety is strategic.

Reason 2: I Want to Be Fully Present

Alcohol is numbing agent.

People drink to:

  • Relax (because they can't naturally)
  • Socialize (because they're anxious sober)
  • Forget (because reality is painful)
  • Feel good (because they don't otherwise)

I want to:

  • Feel everything fully
  • Be comfortable in my own skin
  • Face reality directly
  • Generate my own good feelings

I don't want to escape experience—I want to deepen it.

Reason 3: It's Incompatible with My Practices

Semen retention + alcohol = contradiction

Both are about energy:

  • Retention builds it
  • Alcohol depletes it

Fasting + alcohol = dangerous

Can't purify body while poisoning it.

Meditation + alcohol = pointless

Like vacuuming while dumping dirt.

My spiritual practices require:

  • Clean vessel
  • Clear mind
  • High vibration
  • Maximum energy

Alcohol works against all of this.

Reason 4: I Saw What It Does Long-Term

Working on cruise ships, I saw:

  • Crew drinking every night
  • Aging faster than they should
  • Dreams evaporating
  • Potential unrealized

They weren't bad people.

But alcohol was:

  • Stealing their energy
  • Dulling their edges
  • Making mediocrity comfortable
  • Preventing greatness

I decided: Not me.

The Pushback

People get defensive when you don't drink:

"Oh, so you think you're better than us?"

No.

I think I have specific goals that require specific practices.

You do you. I'll do me.

But I'm not going to pretend alcohol is harmless when my experience says otherwise.

The Benefits I've Experienced

Since quitting:

  • Clearer dreams and better recall
  • Stronger intuition
  • Deeper meditation
  • More energy
  • Better health
  • More money (alcohol is expensive)
  • More time (no hangovers)
  • More pride (keeping commitments to myself)

It was the right choice for me.

ESSAY 5: THE GIFT OF DIFFICULT PEOPLE

Written After Challenging Interaction, Age 28

Difficult people are teachers.

The person who:

  • Triggers you
  • Challenges you
  • Opposes you
  • Irritates you
  • Betrays you

Is showing you where you still have work to do.

The Mirror

Everyone you meet is a mirror.

When someone triggers you, they're showing you:

  • Unhealed wounds
  • Unintegrated shadow
  • Rigid beliefs
  • Ego attachments
  • Areas for growth

The more triggered, the more there is to learn.

My Recent Teacher

Someone recently:

  • Dismissed my work
  • Questioned my competence
  • Implied I'm wasting my time
  • Made me feel small

My first reaction: Anger. Defensiveness. Hurt.

My second reaction: "What is this showing me?"

The lesson: I still seek external validation. I still doubt myself. I still need others to believe in me to fully believe in myself.

The gift: Now I know what to work on.

The Practice

When someone triggers you:

1. Feel it fully Don't suppress the anger/hurt/fear. Let it move through you.

2. Get curious "What about this bothers me so much?" "What belief of mine is being challenged?" "What wound is this touching?"

3. Thank them (silently) They've shown you where you're still stuck.

4. Do the work Heal the wound. Integrate the shadow. Release the belief.

5. Transform the relationship Often, once you've learned the lesson, the difficult person either changes or naturally exits your life.

The Paradox

The people who hurt us most often love us most.

Not in the moment of hurt. But in the cosmic sense.

Our souls made agreements: "I'll trigger you so you can grow." "I'll be the villain in your story so you can become the hero." "I'll challenge you so you become stronger."

Difficult people are doing us a service—even if neither party consciously knows it.

The Gratitude

I'm grateful for:

  • Everyone who rejected me (forced me to develop internal validation)
  • Everyone who betrayed me (taught me discernment)
  • Everyone who criticized me (showed me where to improve)
  • Everyone who doubted me (gave me fuel to prove them wrong—then to prove myself right)

Without them, I'd be weaker.

They were necessary.

ESSAY 6: MONEY AS ENERGY

Written While Developing PVA Bazaar Economic Model, Age 27

Money is not evil. Money is energy.

The question is: What are you doing with it?

The Spiritual Bypass of Poverty

Some spiritual communities glorify poverty:

"Money is root of all evil." "Can't be wealthy and spiritual." "Poverty is more pure."

This is bullshit.

Poverty is:

  • Not more spiritual (just more limited)
  • Not more pure (just more powerless)
  • Not more enlightened (just more constrained)

Poverty doesn't make you holy—it makes you poor.

The Shadow of Wealth

But unchecked wealth pursuit is also trap:

When money becomes:

  • Identity ("I'm worth what I have")
  • Security ("If I get enough, I'll finally be safe")
  • Status ("People will respect me")
  • Escape ("I can buy my way out of problems")

You've made money your god.

And that god demands everything, gives nothing lasting.

The Third Way: Money as Tool

Money is stored life energy.

When you earn money:

  • You gave your life energy (time, effort, creativity)
  • You received stored energy in return (money)
  • You can later exchange that stored energy for what you need

Money is:

  • Medium of exchange (energy flows)
  • Store of value (energy saved)
  • Unit of account (energy measured)

That's it.

Not good or evil. Just energy.

The Ethical Question

What are you doing with this energy?

Are you:

  • Hoarding it (energy stagnates)
  • Using it to exploit (energy corrupts)
  • Wasting it on meaningless consumption (energy dissipates)

Or:

  • Circulating it (energy flows)
  • Using it to create value (energy multiplies)
  • Investing it in what matters (energy transforms)

This is the difference.

The PVA Approach

We need money to:

  • Build Ziggurat Hubs (physical infrastructure)
  • Develop technology (Bioharmonic Suit, etc.)
  • Support community (people need to eat)
  • Fund research (consciousness science, etc.)
  • Create systems (PVA Bazaar, etc.)

We're not anti-money. We're anti-exploitation.

We're not rejecting wealth. We're rejecting wealth at others' expense.

We want:

  • Abundance for all
  • Fair exchange
  • Prosperity with purpose
  • Money serving life, not life serving money

The Affirmation

"Money flows to me easily and abundantly. I use it wisely and lovingly. I circulate it generously. I create value for others. I am worthy of prosperity. My wealth serves my purpose."

This is right relationship with money.

ESSAY 7: THE COURAGE TO BE DISLIKED

Written After Public Criticism, Age 28

You cannot live authentically and be universally liked.

Impossible.

Why People-Pleasing Fails

When you try to please everyone:

  • You fragment yourself
  • You lose your center
  • You betray your truth
  • You exhaust yourself

And you still don't succeed.

Because:

  • Different people want different things from you
  • Your authentic self threatens those living inauthentically
  • Your light triggers others' darkness
  • Your choices challenge others' choices

No matter what you do, someone will dislike you.

The Liberation

Once you accept this:

You're free.

Free to:

  • Be yourself fully
  • Speak your truth
  • Make unpopular choices
  • Follow your calling
  • Disappoint people

Not because you're cruel.

But because you're authentic.

The Criteria

The only question that matters:

"Am I aligned with my highest self?"

Not:

  • "Will people like this?"
  • "Will this be popular?"
  • "Will this offend anyone?"

But:

  • "Is this true?"
  • "Is this loving?"
  • "Is this mine to do?"

If yes to those three, proceed regardless of others' opinions.

The Peace

There's profound peace in:

Knowing some people will dislike you. Not needing to change their minds. Letting them be wrong about you. Focusing on those who do understand.

Your tribe gets it. Others don't need to.

The Practice

When criticized or disliked:

1. Check if critique has merit Is there truth in it? Can I learn from this? Am I in denial about something?

2. If yes: Integrate and improve

3. If no: Release and continue

4. Either way: Don't let it derail you

You have work to do. Don't let others' opinions stop you.

ESSAY 8: SEASONS OF THE SOUL

Written During Transition Period, Age 27

Life moves in seasons.

Just like nature:

  • Spring (new beginnings, growth, optimism)
  • Summer (fullness, abundance, activity)
  • Fall (harvest, letting go, transition)
  • Winter (rest, introspection, death before rebirth)

Souls have seasons too.

Recognizing Your Season

Spring of the Soul:

  • New ideas flowing
  • Energy high
  • Possibilities everywhere
  • Enthusiasm abundant
  • Time to plant seeds

Summer of the Soul:

  • Projects thriving
  • Connections deepening
  • Productivity high
  • Time to build and expand

Fall of the Soul:

  • Reaping what you've sown
  • Releasing what didn't work
  • Gratitude for lessons
  • Time to harvest and prepare

Winter of the Soul:

  • Energy low
  • Motivation scarce
  • Everything feels hard
  • Time to rest and restore

The Mistake We Make

We try to force summer all year.

Modern culture says:

  • Always be productive
  • Never stop hustling
  • Constant growth required
  • Rest is laziness

This is madness.

Even nature rests.

Trees drop leaves. Bears hibernate. Seeds lie dormant.

If you never winter, you'll never spring.

Honoring Winter

When you're in winter:

Don't fight it. Don't force productivity. Don't judge yourself.

Instead:

  • Rest deeply
  • Reflect quietly
  • Read and absorb
  • Process and integrate
  • Trust the dormancy

The seeds are germinating underground. You can't see it, but growth is happening.

My Current Season

I'm between fall and winter:

Harvesting insights from hermit years. Releasing old identities. Preparing for spring's manifestation. Resting before the work intensifies.

It feels slow.

But I trust:

  • Winter is necessary
  • Spring will come
  • The cycle continues
  • Each season has purpose

For You

Whatever season you're in:

Honor it.

Don't rush spring. Don't resist winter. Don't cling to summer. Don't skip fall.

Each season teaches. Each season prepares. Each season matters.

Trust your season.

CONCLUSION: THE MOSAIC OF WISDOM

These essays, written at different times for different reasons, form a mosaic.

Each piece reflects light differently. Each offers a specific medicine. Each speaks to a particular moment.

Together they say:

Be authentic, even if disliked. Honor your intensity. Trust your loneliness is temporary. Use money as tool, not master. Learn from difficult people. Stay sober and conscious. Don't seek validation. Honor your season.

This is the path.

Not neat and linear. But spiral and fractal. Returning to center from different angles, seeing new facets each time.

May these fragments serve whoever needs them.
