🧍 Physical Balance Practices

1. The Wall Test

Put yourself on the back of a straight wall. Heels, shoulder blades, lower back, and back of skull must touch. Keep that to improve.

Why it matters: Your entire alignment depends on spinal symmetry. Most of our imbalances start here.

2. Breathing with Both Nostrils

Teach children to breathe with both nostrils. If one is plugged, unplug it quickly. Or plug the other one? Neither of those is correct—those are "this or that."

The balanced way: Unplug the nose, then plug the other nostril and have them breathe the same amount on the other one. This regains slow blood flow from not having as much oxygen pushed over there. Continue life like that to retain balance.

3. Chewing Equally

Make sure you chew one day left and one day right to keep structure of bone and symmetry. When you chew only on one side, one side of your face grows stronger while the other atrophies.

Result: Facial asymmetry, jaw misalignment, and imbalance throughout your entire body.

4. Strengthen Your Weak Eye

Use an eye patch on your dominant eye to strengthen the weaker one. We overcompensate with our stronger eye, leaving the other underdeveloped.

Goal: Equal visual strength = equal brain hemisphere activation.

5. Stop Perma-Flexing

Stop permanently flexing one side of your body. You're permanently flexing one side for strength while the other side never gets used. One side is fatter because it grew but was never worked out. One side is weaker and can't hold the same resistance, and elasticity gets rigid.

Result: You lose facial symmetry if you don't find BALANCE.

6. Workout Balance

When we lift heavy, we overcompensate our weaker muscles with our stronger ones, therefore not being balanced and striving for your own perfection. We do the same thing with everything else in life as well.

Solution: Be skinny, then get big. Never dirty bulk. You won't get complete peak physical condition by overcompensating.

7. The Pee Test

When we have to pee really bad, we subconsciously hold all the liquid on the stronger side of our stomach. If we're tilting one way, that's the strong side of your core.

That relaxing, stress-free feeling you get during your pee? That's your back going back to its neutral position and resting. Your back is at complete ease for those 5-10 seconds.
